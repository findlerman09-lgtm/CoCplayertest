background: >-
  Clara Mercer is the daughter of a London compositor and learned early that newspapers are made by ordinary people working against deadlines. She entered journalism through short local items, descriptive reporting, interviews, and subjects considered suitable for a woman writer. In a newsroom where routine district and crime reporting was ordinarily men's work, her harder East-End assignments were unusual and hard-won. She earned them by verifying names, tracing accounts to their sources, correcting mistakes, and returning with usable copy.
history: >-
  Clara once covered a warehouse fire in Wapping that Arthur Bell inspected professionally. The public explanation omitted problems Bell could not freely discuss. Bell corrected one technical error in Clara's understanding; Clara checked rather than defending her copy and published a correction. She did not print the larger allegation she could not substantiate. The lesson stayed with her: know which sentence is yours, which belongs to a source, and which nobody has actually proved.
